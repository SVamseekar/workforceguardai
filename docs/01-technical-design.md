## 1. Summary

WorkforceGuard AI is a single-tenant analytics application that helps EU
employers (500–5,000 headcount) prepare for the EU Pay Transparency
Directive: member-state transposition by 7 June 2026, then employer
reports on 2026 data due in 2027. The product compares a customer's
internal pay and workforce data against official EU labour-market sources
(Eurostat, Eurofound, EIGE, EURES) and produces a signed evidence pack
the customer attaches to their compliance filing.

This document defines how v1.0 is built. It is the engineering contract for
the next six weeks. Anything not specified here is out of scope for v1.0.

The shape of the system is intentionally narrow:

- **One paying customer at a time per deployment.** No multi-tenant code
  paths in v1.0.
- **Deterministic numeric layer.** Every number rendered to the user is
  computed in SQL inside a dbt model and read directly by the API. No model
  inference is on the path between data and a numeric claim.
- **All data is public and citable.** No scraping, no closed sources, no
  synthetic numbers leaking into customer-facing claims. The full source
  list is in §4.1.
- **Auditability is the product.** An evidence pack is a hash-signed,
  PDF-rendered, fully-provenanced JSON document that the customer staples to
  their filing.

## 2. Context and motivation

### 2.1 What we are building, in one paragraph

A workforce-intelligence dashboard and evidence-pack generator for EU
compliance. The customer uploads their payroll and job architecture; the
system enriches it with public EU labour data (Eurostat, EU-SILC, EWCS,
EURES, EIGE), computes pay-transparency metrics, and emits a signed evidence
pack. A governance layer logs every approval, override, and export with a
tamper-evident hash chain.

### 2.2 Why now

The EU Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970) has a transposition deadline of
**7 June 2026**. Employers with 100+ workers must publish pay gaps by
worker category and justify any unadjusted gap above 5% with objective
gender-neutral criteria. The first reports are due in 2027 for 2026 data.

Three concrete consequences for the design:

1. The first cohort of buyers must collect 2026 data **as it happens**, not
   reconstruct it later. They need the product live in their environment by
   summer 2026.
2. The output is a regulated artefact. "Looks roughly right" is not enough;
   numbers must be reconcilable to a public source line by line.
3. Buyers are CPOs and Heads of Compensation, advised by employment counsel.
   They are unforgiving of magic numbers and forgiving of plain UIs.

### 2.3 Goals (v1.0)

- **G1.** Ingest a customer's payroll snapshot and job architecture under a
  documented schema, with validation and clear error reporting.
- **G2.** Ingest 16 named Eurostat datasets, ESCO occupation/skill
  reference, EU-SILC indicators, and (new in v1.0) EURES vacancy statistics
  and EIGE Gender Equality Index, refreshed on a documented cadence.
- **G3.** Produce a workforce overview that compares the customer to the
  EU-27 average and to a comparator country/sector with full provenance on
  every metric.
- **G4.** Generate a signed evidence pack (JSON + PDF) that a customer can
  hand to legal counsel and that can be regenerated byte-identically from
  the same warehouse vintage.
- **G5.** Log every governance action (approve, override, reverse, export)
  to a tamper-evident store with actor identity.
- **G6.** Deploy in an EU region (Hetzner FSN, OVH GRA, Scaleway PAR, or
  AWS eu-central-1) with TLS, OIDC login, daily backups, and a written
  restore procedure.

### 2.4 Non-goals (v1.0)

- Multi-tenant isolation. One deployment per customer.
- LLM-generated narratives or chat. The narrative layer is templated SQL
  output. The LLM design lives in §6.4 as a v1.1 bet.
- Mobile app or responsive design below 1024px. Compliance work is desktop.
- Real-time streaming. Daily refresh is the contract; quarterly is the
  effective Eurostat cadence for most series.
- The Phase 4 approval workflow (route an evidence pack through a chain of
  approvers). Spec for this is captured by observing the design partner's
  first real filing in 2027 (and the rehearsal cycle in summer 2026), not
  invented up front.
- Phase 5 AI copilot.
- Public SaaS / self-serve signup.

### 2.5 Audience for this document

Engineers building or reviewing v1.0. A reader is expected to know FastAPI,
React, SQL, and dbt at a working level, and to have read
`docs/technical-assessment.md` once.

## 3. System overview

### 3.1 Architectural shape

```
                ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                │  Public EU sources (HTTPS, no auth)          │
                │  Eurostat · ESCO · EU-SILC · EURES · EIGE    │
                │  · Eurofound EWCS · ILOSTAT (reconciliation) │
                └─────────────────────┬────────────────────────┘
                                      │ scheduled pulls (cron)
                                      ▼
                ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                │  Raw zone (versioned files on EU disk)       │
                │  data/eu_raw/  data/reference_raw/           │
                │  one parquet/csv per (dataset, vintage)      │
                └─────────────────────┬────────────────────────┘
                                      │
   customer CSV ─────────────────────►│
   (payroll, job architecture)        │
                                      ▼
                ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                │  Warehouse: DuckDB (per customer)            │
                │  dbt project: staging → marts                │
                │  Provenance carried on every row             │
                └─────────────────────┬────────────────────────┘
                                      │ read-only handle
                                      ▼
                ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                │  FastAPI service (single process)            │
                │  api/ · domain/ · repository/ · policy/      │
                │  Governance log: SQLite, hash-chained        │
                │  Evidence pack: JSON + PDF, signed           │
                └─────────────────────┬────────────────────────┘
                                      │ HTTPS, OIDC-authenticated
                                      ▼
                ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                │  React SPA (TypeScript, TanStack Query)      │
                │  Pages: Overview · Compare · Evidence ·      │
                │         Governance                           │
                └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Each box is one process or one storage layer. There is no message bus, no
cache layer, no separate worker. The only out-of-process scheduled work is
cron jobs that run the ingestion scripts.

### 3.2 Tenancy and deployment unit

The unit of deployment is **one customer, one Linux VM, one DuckDB file,
one FastAPI process, one Caddy reverse proxy in front**. This stays true
through v1.0. The migration trigger to a multi-tenant Postgres deployment
is the third paying customer signing, not the second; see §7.4.

### 3.3 Boundaries and trust

Three trust boundaries:

1. **Public-internet → ingestion.** Eurostat, ESCO, etc. We trust the
   payload up to schema validation (see §4.4), not beyond. Reconciliation
   tests assert the headline number we computed equals the headline the
   publisher shows.
2. **Customer CSV → warehouse.** The customer is trusted to upload their
   own data, but we never display a customer-specific claim until that
   data has cleared the trust gate (§4.6).
3. **Browser → API.** OIDC-authenticated. The browser is treated as
   hostile until the OIDC token verifies.

There is no fourth boundary in v1.0 because there is no LLM. When the LLM
ships in v1.1, the model never sees raw customer payroll; it only sees
aggregates that have already cleared the trust gate.

## 4. Data flow

This section walks one number from source to screen.

### 4.1 The path of a single metric (worked example: gender pay gap)

We follow the rendered claim *"Your unadjusted gender pay gap is 11.4%, vs
12.7% in DE finance, vs 12.0% EU-27 average (Eurostat earn_gr_gpgr2,
2024 vintage, pulled 2026-04-15)."*

```
Step 1  Eurostat publishes earn_gr_gpgr2 (annual, NACE Rev. 2, by sector).
Step 2  scripts/pull_eu_data.py runs nightly. For each dataset in
        configs/eu_sources.yaml, it pulls JSON-stat, attaches
        (source_dataset_code, source_url, source_vintage,
        source_pulled_at, licence), and writes to data/eu_raw/<code>/
        <pulled_at>.parquet.
Step 3  scripts/build_phase1_workspace.py invokes `dbt build`. The model
        analytics/models/staging/eurostat/stg_eurostat__gender_pay_gap_sector.sql
        normalises wide JSON-stat into long format and preserves provenance
        columns.
Step 4  analytics/models/marts/core/mart_semantic_metrics.sql joins the
        staging row to dim_geography, dim_sector, dim_date, and
        dim_metric_registry to produce one row per
        (metric_id, geography_id, sector_id, period_id) with the value
        and full provenance.
Step 5  The customer's payroll snapshot (data/internal_raw/payroll/...)
        flows through stg_internal__payroll_snapshot.sql into
        fct_internal_pay_snapshot.sql. mart_internal_market_pay_benchmark.sql
        computes the customer's unadjusted gap and joins it to the matching
        Eurostat benchmark row.
Step 6  policy/trust.py checks data_trust_level on the customer rows.
        If level < customer_reconciled, the customer-specific claim is
        suppressed and only the Eurostat figure is exposed.
Step 7  repository/warehouse.py executes a single SQL read from
        mart_internal_market_pay_benchmark for the requested filter state.
Step 8  domain/metrics.py wraps the result in a typed MetricView; tone
        ("good"/"watch") is derived from dim_metric_registry thresholds,
        not hardcoded.
Step 9  domain/narratives.py renders the templated sentence using the
        MetricView. No string ever embeds a number that did not come from
        the MetricView.
Step 10 api/overview.py shapes the response; main.py adds request id,
        structured logs, and freshness headers.
Step 11 React: hooks/useOverview.ts fetches via TanStack Query; the
        Overview page renders the metric, the comparison, and the
        provenance chip linking to the evidence drawer.
```

The point of the walkthrough: the number traverses **one** computation
path. There is no place where a number is rounded, re-sourced, or
regenerated by something other than the warehouse.

### 4.2 Refresh cadence

| Source | Cadence | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Eurostat (16 datasets) | Daily 03:00 UTC | cron in `scripts/pull_eu_data.py` |
| ESCO occupation/skills | Weekly Sunday 04:00 UTC | cron |
| EU-SILC indicators | Daily (Eurostat publishes annually but we pull daily to detect updates) | cron |
| EURES vacancy statistics | Daily 03:30 UTC | cron (new in v1.0) |
| EIGE Gender Equality Index | Weekly Sunday 04:30 UTC | cron (new in v1.0) |
| Eurofound EWCS extracts | Manual on each report release (5-yearly + interim) | runbook |
| Customer payroll | On customer push, or weekly | manual / SFTP drop |

If a pull fails, the previous vintage stays live and the UI surfaces a
freshness warning past the documented SLA window (35 days for quarterly
sources, 400 days for annual sources).

### 4.3 What "fresh" means in the UI

Every page header carries a freshness pill: *"As of 2026-04-26, 02:00 UTC.
EU sources current through 2024 Q4."* Clicking the pill opens the data
sources panel, listing each source, its vintage, and its `pulled_at`.

### 4.4 Schema validation

Three layers, fail-fast:

1. **Ingestion-time schema check.** The pull script asserts the JSON-stat
   dimension list matches a checked-in baseline at
   `configs/source_schemas/<dataset_code>.json`. A new or removed
   dimension fails the build, does not silently produce a different
   dataset.
2. **dbt schema tests.** `not_null` and `unique` on every mart's primary
   key, plus a custom test that asserts every row in
   `mart_semantic_metrics` has all six provenance fields populated.
3. **API contract test.** A pytest suite asserts the JSON shape returned
   by `/api/overview` matches a versioned JSON Schema in
   `dashboard/backend/contracts/`.

### 4.5 Customer-data ingestion

The customer hands over two files:

- `payroll_snapshot.csv` (period, employee_id, gross_annual_eur, fte,
  gender, job_code, country, hire_date, termination_date)
- `job_architecture.csv` (job_code, job_family, job_level, job_title,
  career_track, esco_occupation_code)

We never see PII beyond what is required for these two files. `employee_id`
is treated as a pseudonymous identifier; the customer is responsible for
the mapping back to a real person.

The ingest path is:

1. SFTP drop or signed-URL upload to `data/internal_raw/<customer_id>/
   <pulled_at>/`.
2. `scripts/prepare_internal_company_data.py` validates against a JSON
   Schema, writes a per-row error report, and refuses the snapshot if the
   error rate exceeds 0.5%.
3. The validated snapshot is loaded into the staging models and
   reconciliation runs: row count, total comp, headcount must match the
   customer's stated totals within 0.1% or `data_trust_level` stays at
   `customer_unverified`.

### 4.6 The trust gate

`data_trust_level` is an enum on every internal fact row, with three
values:

- `sample` — fixture data shipped with the repo. Never surfaced.
- `customer_unverified` — real customer data that has not yet reconciled.
  Visible to the customer in a "preview" view, but no claims are computed
  off it and no evidence pack will include it.
- `customer_reconciled` — passed reconciliation, signed off by the
  customer's data owner via a one-click "I confirm these totals" action
  that is logged to the governance store.

Only `customer_reconciled` rows feed company-specific claims. The gate is
enforced in `policy/trust.py` and is the only place in the codebase that
reads the customer-data flag. If anything else looks at it, that's a bug.

## 5. Component design

### 5.1 Data layer

#### 5.1.1 Sources of truth

| Source | Code reference | Used for | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eurostat JSON-stat API | `configs/eu_sources.yaml` (16 datasets) | Employment, unemployment, vacancy, pay gap, flows, slack, EU-SILC, GDP per capita, commuting | CC-BY 4.0 |
| ESCO API | `scripts/pull_esco_api_data.py` | Occupation hierarchy, skills, alternative titles | EUPL / CC-BY |
| EURES vacancy statistics | new in v1.0, `scripts/pull_eures_data.py` | Live demand by occupation × region | EU open data |
| EIGE Gender Equality Index | new in v1.0, `scripts/pull_eige_data.py` | Pay-equity context, country power-domain score | CC-BY 4.0 |
| Eurofound EWCS 2024 | manual extract → `data/reference_raw/ewcs/` | Job-quality narrative, citable language only | CC-BY 4.0 |
| ILOSTAT | reconciliation only, `tests/reconciliation/` | Cross-check vs Eurostat headlines | CC-BY 4.0 |
| NACE Rev. 2, ISCO-08 | seeds in `analytics/seeds/` | Dimensional reference | EU public |

Sources explicitly excluded from the warehouse: consulting reports
(Deloitte, McKinsey, BCG, Bain, EY, PwC, Mercer), LinkedIn / Glassdoor /
Levels.fyi, and any scraped feed. These remain citable in narrative text
where useful, but no number from them lands in a row of a fact table.

#### 5.1.2 Warehouse: dbt project layout

```
analytics/
├── dbt_project.yml
├── seeds/                # NACE, ISCO, country reference codes
├── models/
│   ├── staging/
│   │   ├── eurostat/     # 1 model per dataset code
│   │   ├── eures/        # new in v1.0
│   │   ├── eige/         # new in v1.0
│   │   └── internal/     # payroll, job_architecture, hris, ats, learning
│   └── marts/
│       ├── core/         # dim_geography, dim_sector, dim_date, dim_occupation,
│       │                 # fct_labour_market_region_sector,
│       │                 # mart_semantic_metrics, mart_workforce_command_center
│       ├── internal/     # fct_internal_*, mart_internal_market_pay_benchmark,
│       │                 # mart_company_decision_support
│       └── reference/    # dim_data_sources, dim_governance_actions,
│                         # dim_metric_registry
└── tests/
    ├── generic/          # provenance_complete, trust_level_valid
    └── reconciliation/   # eurostat_headline_unemployment, gender_pay_gap, etc.
```

New work in v1.0 vs the audit baseline:

- **`dim_date`** and a documented period grain on every fact.
- **`dim_occupation`** as a recursive dim over ESCO with parent_key + level.
- **Surrogate keys** via `dbt_utils.generate_surrogate_key` on dims; SCD-2
  on `dim_sector` and `dim_geography`.
- **Provenance columns** propagated through every staging and mart model,
  enforced by a generic test `provenance_complete`.
- **`data_trust_level`** column on every internal fact, enforced by a
  generic test `trust_level_valid`.

#### 5.1.3 Metric registry

`dim_metric_registry` is the only place tone thresholds, units, display
names, and formatting hints live. Each row:

```
metric_id            text   pk        e.g. 'unemployment_rate'
display_name         text             'Unemployment rate'
unit                 text             '%' | 'score' | 'count' | 'eur'
direction_good       text             'lower' | 'higher' | 'target'
watch_threshold      numeric          e.g. 8.0 for unemployment
target_value         numeric          nullable
description_md       text             one-paragraph definition
default_source_id    text   fk        dim_data_sources.source_id
```

The API never decides whether a metric is doing well or badly. The
warehouse decides, the API serves the decision, the UI renders it.

#### 5.1.4 Provenance contract

Every numeric row in any mart carries:

```
source_publisher     text not null   'Eurostat'
source_dataset_code  text not null   'earn_gr_gpgr2'
source_url           text not null   full URL to dataset landing page
source_vintage       text not null   '2024' or '2024Q4'
source_pulled_at     timestamp utc not null
licence              text not null   'CC-BY 4.0'
```

A row missing any of these six fails the build. The API exposes them on
every metric in the response payload. The UI shows them in the evidence
drawer and embeds them in the evidence-pack PDF.

#### 5.2.2 Endpoint surface (v1.0)

| Method | Path | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| GET | `/health` | service ok + git sha + warehouse vintage |
| GET | `/api/sources` | list of `dim_data_sources` rows |
| GET | `/api/freshness` | per-source `pulled_at` and SLA status |
| GET | `/api/overview` | metrics + comparisons + narratives for filter state |
| GET | `/api/overview/{metric_id}` | single metric, full provenance |

| GET | `/api/evidence-pack?format=json` | signed JSON pack |
| GET | `/api/evidence-pack?format=pdf` | rendered PDF |
| POST | `/api/governance-events` | record action; appends to hash chain |
| GET | `/api/governance-events` | paged list of events |
| POST | `/api/customer-data/reconcile` | confirm totals, flips trust level to `customer_reconciled` |

All endpoints behind OIDC (`Authorization: Bearer <token>`). `/health` and
`/` are unauthenticated and reveal nothing sensitive.

#### 5.2.3 Governance event log

A SQLite database next to the DuckDB file. WAL mode. One table:

```sql
CREATE TABLE governance_events (
  event_id        TEXT PRIMARY KEY,            -- uuid v4
  occurred_at     TEXT NOT NULL,               -- iso8601 utc
  actor_email     TEXT NOT NULL,               -- from OIDC token
  action_code     TEXT NOT NULL,               -- 'approve' | 'override' | 'reverse' | 'export'
  target_type     TEXT NOT NULL,               -- 'metric' | 'evidence_pack' | 'customer_reconciliation'
  target_id       TEXT NOT NULL,
  reason          TEXT,
  context_json    TEXT NOT NULL,               -- canonicalised
  prev_event_hash TEXT NOT NULL,               -- hash of previous row, or '' for genesis
  event_hash      TEXT NOT NULL                -- sha256(canonical(self) || prev_event_hash)
);
CREATE INDEX gov_events_occurred_at ON governance_events(occurred_at);
```

The hash chain is checked on read by a periodic verifier and on every
evidence-pack export. A break in the chain blocks export and pages the
operator.

This is **tamper-evident**, not tamper-proof. Tamper-proof requires a
write-once medium and is overbuild for v1.0; the chain plus daily off-site
backup is the right level of paranoia for one customer.

#### 5.2.4 Evidence pack

Two formats, same content:

**JSON pack (`/api/evidence-pack?format=json`):**

```json
{
  "pack_id": "uuid",
  "generated_at": "2026-05-12T09:14:22Z",
  "generated_by": "anna.schmidt@design-partner.eu",
  "warehouse_vintage": {
    "dbt_run_id": "...",
    "sources": [
      {"source_dataset_code": "earn_gr_gpgr2", "vintage": "2024", "pulled_at": "..."},
      ...
    ]
  },
  "filters": { "country": "DE", "sector": "K_FINANCE", "period": "2024" },
  "claims": [
    {
      "claim_id": "...",
      "metric_id": "gender_pay_gap",
      "value": 11.4,
      "unit": "%",
      "comparison": { "geography": "EU27_AVG", "value": 12.0 },
      "provenance": {
        "source_publisher": "Eurostat",
        "source_dataset_code": "earn_gr_gpgr2",
        "source_url": "https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/earn_gr_gpgr2",
        "source_vintage": "2024",
        "source_pulled_at": "2026-04-15T03:02:11Z",
        "licence": "CC-BY 4.0"
      }
    }
  ],
  "narrative_md": "...",
  "claim_set_hash": "sha256:...",
  "signature": "ed25519:..."
}
```

`claim_set_hash` is sha256 over the canonicalised claim list (sorted
keys, no whitespace). The signature uses an ed25519 keypair held in the
deployment's secret store.

**PDF pack:** rendered server-side with WeasyPrint from a single HTML
template at `dashboard/backend/templates/evidence_pack.html`. Last page
embeds the `claim_set_hash` and `signature` as text and as a QR code that
encodes the verification URL.

Regenerating a pack from the same warehouse vintage and filter state
produces a byte-identical JSON pack and a PDF with identical content
(timestamps in PDF are pinned to `generated_at` for determinism).

#### 5.2.5 Authn / authz

- **Authentication:** OIDC against the customer's IdP (Azure AD or Okta).
  We accept a JWT in the `Authorization` header; we verify signature,
  audience, expiry, and that the email domain is in the deployment's
  allowlist.
- **Authorisation:** one role in v1.0, `analyst`. Anyone authenticated can
  read overviews and generate evidence packs. The "who approved this"
  question is answered by the governance log, not by separate roles.
- **Service-to-service:** ingestion scripts run as a system user under
  cron, with no API access. They write directly to the filesystem and the
  warehouse.

#### 5.2.6 Logging and observability

- Structured JSON logs via `structlog`.
- Every request carries an `X-Request-ID` (generated if absent) that is
  echoed in every log line and the response header.
- `/health` returns `{ok, git_sha, warehouse_vintage, last_dbt_run_at}` so
  an external uptime monitor can detect both process death and data
  staleness.
- Errors are mapped through one `guarded()` boundary that masks file paths
  in the response and logs the full traceback server-side with the request
  id.

### 5.3 Frontend

#### 5.3.1 Stack

- React 18, Vite, **TypeScript**.
- TanStack Query for the API layer (cache, retry, dedupe, stale-while-
  revalidate).
- Recharts for charts, wrapped behind one `<MetricChart>` component that
  picks line vs bar from the metric registry.
- Lucide for icons.
- No CSS-in-JS framework. Hand-rolled CSS with CSS variables for theming.
  Compliance buyers do not care about Tailwind; we care about bundle size
  and predictability.

```
├── hooks/                         # useFilters, useOverview, useEvidencePack
├── api/                           # one typed fetcher per backend route
├── lib/                           # formatters, period helpers, locale
└── types/                         # generated from backend contracts/*.json
```

The single `Overview.jsx` (2,041 lines) is dissolved into pages and
primitives.

#### 5.3.3 Type safety end-to-end

The backend's `contracts/*.json` JSON Schemas are the source of truth.
A pre-commit hook regenerates `frontend/src/types/api.ts` via
`json-schema-to-typescript`. A CI job fails if the types drift.

#### 5.3.4 Empty / loading / error states

One `<DataState>` wrapper handles all three. The default UX:

- **Loading:** skeleton chart + skeleton metric tiles. No spinner.
- **Empty:** explanatory message naming the filter state and a "clear
  filters" action.
- **Error:** the request id, a copy button, a "report" link. No raw
  exception text.

#### 5.3.5 Localisation

- Number and date formatting respects `Accept-Language`, defaults to
  `en-IE` for English EU readers.
- Evidence packs render in the customer's chosen language (DE, FR, ES, IT,
  EN). The narrative templates are translated at build time, stored as
  fluent (`.ftl`) files, and the same numeric data renders identically
  across languages.

#### 5.3.6 Accessibility

axe-core runs in CI on every page. WCAG 2.1 AA is the target. This is
checked because EU procurement teams check it, not for marketing.

### 5.4 The "AI" surface

#### 5.4.1 What ships in v1.0

Templated narratives. `domain/narratives.py` takes a `MetricView` (a typed
object with the metric, its provenance, the comparison value, and the
tone) and renders one of a small set of sentence templates. Every numeric
token in the output is read from the `MetricView`. The function is a pure
function with no model call.

Marketing copy must reflect this. We do not call this AI in v1.0. We call
it "explainable analyst narratives". When asked, the answer is: "There is
no model in the loop. Every number comes from a public source; every
sentence is generated from those numbers and a small library of templates
that an analyst can read."

#### 5.4.2 What we build the *substrate* for in v1.0, ship in v1.1

We build `domain/metrics.py` so that it doubles as a **tool surface** for
a future LLM. Each function is a typed, side-effect-free call:

```python
def get_metric(metric_id: MetricId, filters: FilterState) -> MetricView: ...
def compare(filters_a: FilterState, filters_b: FilterState,
            metric_id: MetricId) -> ComparisonView: ...
def list_available_metrics() -> list[MetricRegistryEntry]: ...
def cite(source_id: SourceId) -> SourceCitation: ...
```

These functions are what `/api/overview` and `/api/ask` already call.
They are also what an LLM, in v1.1, will call as Anthropic tool definitions.

#### 5.4.3 The v1.1 LLM design (not built yet, locked here so v1.0 doesn't make it impossible)

- **Provider:** Anthropic Claude. Opus 4.7 for narrative drafting and the
  pay-transparency PDF summary page. Haiku 4.5 for cheap follow-up
  classification.
- **Surface:** the four typed tools above. The model is not given direct
  warehouse access, ever.
- **Verification:** before any model output is returned to the user, a
  verifier function asserts every numeric token in the output appears in
  the tool-call results. Mismatches fail closed with a templated fallback.
- **Caching:** prompt caching on the system prompt and the metric registry
  table. The cache hit rate is the cost lever.
- **Audit:** every model call writes
  `(prompt_hash, response_hash, tool_calls, cost_usd, latency_ms)` to the
  governance log.

The reason this is in this document and not the v1.1 doc: the v1.0
backend split (api/domain/repository/policy) is shaped to make this LLM
addition a low-risk increment. If the split is right, adding the LLM is
a new module under `domain/` that calls the existing tools. If the split
is wrong, the LLM addition becomes another monolith.

## 6. Trade-offs and alternatives considered

We considered each of the following alternatives explicitly. For each, the
chosen path is at the top, the alternatives are below with the reason for
rejection.

### 6.1 Warehouse: DuckDB (chosen) vs Postgres vs Snowflake/BigQuery

**Chosen: DuckDB on local disk, one file per customer.**

- One process reads the same file the dbt project writes. Zero network
  hop, sub-millisecond reads on the working set.
- 9.7 MB today, expected ceiling ~2 GB at v1.0 scope. Comfortable headroom
  before any hardware question arises.
- Deterministic: a `dbt build` against a fixed input produces a
  byte-identical file, which is what evidence-pack regeneration depends
  on.
- Backups are a file copy.

**Rejected: Postgres in v1.0.**
Postgres becomes correct when (a) we have multiple customers in one
deployment, or (b) we need concurrent writers, or (c) we need
point-in-time recovery beyond a daily snapshot. None of these are true at
v1.0. Migrating to Postgres is a one-week task triggered by customer #3;
see §7.2.

**Rejected: Snowflake / BigQuery.**
EU data residency is achievable on both, but the per-tenant cost floor and
operational surface area (warehouse sizing, egress) are wrong for a
product that runs analytics over <1 GB of data per customer. We would
adopt one of these only if we shipped a true SaaS multi-tenant product
serving 50+ customers, which is not on the v1.0 path.

### 6.2 Narrative layer: templates (chosen) vs LLM-generated vs LLM-with-tools

**Chosen: templated narratives in v1.0; LLM-with-tools in v1.1.**

A compliance product fails closed on hallucinations. A templated layer
cannot hallucinate by construction: every numeric token is a typed read
from a typed object. The cost is generic-sounding sentences, which the
buyer trades willingly for defensibility.

**Rejected: LLM-generated narratives in v1.0.**
Even with Anthropic's tool-use, the verification layer (every number in
output must appear in tool results) is non-trivial to build, and the
failure mode of getting it wrong is "we shipped a wrong number to a
regulator". Wrong order: prove the product works deterministically with
templates, then add the LLM as a re-write layer in v1.1 once we have a
real corpus of customer narrative preferences.

**Rejected: LLM-only with no tools.**
A model that sees raw warehouse rows and emits prose is the wrong
architecture for a regulated artefact. Not built, not planned.

### 6.3 PDF rendering: WeasyPrint (chosen) vs headless Chromium vs LaTeX

**Chosen: WeasyPrint, in-process.**

- Pure Python, ships in the same container as the API.
- CSS Paged Media support is enough for the evidence-pack template.
- Deterministic output when fonts are pinned and timestamps are explicit.

**Rejected: headless Chromium / Playwright.**
Better CSS fidelity, but a separate process with a 200+ MB footprint,
GPU/driver friction in containers, and a security surface we do not
need. The evidence pack is a structured document, not a marketing
brochure.

**Rejected: LaTeX.**
Best typography, worst accessibility for engineers maintaining it. We are
not staffed to maintain a LaTeX template alongside an HTML one.

### 6.4 Authentication: customer OIDC (chosen) vs username/password vs magic links

**Chosen: OIDC against the customer's IdP.**

EU enterprise buyers expect SSO. A login form with passwords reads as
unserious to a procurement team. OIDC is one library, one config block,
one redirect URL.

**Rejected: username + password.**
Increases breach blast radius, and we would have to build password reset,
MFA, lockout. The same engineering buys nothing the customer wants.

**Rejected: magic-link email.**
Acceptable for early-pilot bypass, not for a product that serves a
compliance role. Reserved for an emergency break-glass.

### 6.5 Frontend: React SPA (chosen) vs server-rendered (Next.js / Remix) vs HTMX

**Chosen: a thin React SPA with TanStack Query.**

The product is not SEO-sensitive. The total page count is small. The
state model is "filters drive everything"; SPA + query cache fits this
exactly.

**Rejected: Next.js / Remix.**
Server rendering buys cold-start performance and SEO. We need neither.
The cost is two runtimes and an SSR cache that must be reasoned about.

**Rejected: HTMX over server-rendered HTML.**
Tempting for a product this small. The reason we did not pick it: the
chart layer (Recharts) is React-native, and re-implementing it on top of
SVG-from-the-server is more work than we save by dropping the React
runtime.

### 6.6 Tenancy: single-tenant per VM (chosen) vs schema-per-tenant vs row-level security

**Chosen: single-tenant per VM in v1.0.**

The unit of isolation is the VM. There is no chance of cross-customer
data leak by code defect because there is no cross-customer code path.

**Deferred: schema-per-tenant in Postgres (v1.1.5, on customer #3).**
The right shape once the customer count exceeds the operational cost of
running N VMs. We will not write any v1.0 code that assumes this shape.

**Rejected: row-level-security as the primary isolation.**
Postgres RLS is a fine secondary defence, but using it as the primary
boundary is asking for a single missing `WHERE` clause to leak everything.
For HR data this is an unacceptable failure mode.

### 6.7 Customer data ingest: SFTP + signed-URL upload (chosen) vs API push vs cloud-storage shared bucket

**Chosen: SFTP drop or signed-URL upload to per-customer
`data/internal_raw/<customer_id>/<pulled_at>/`.**

- Familiar for HR-data teams. Their existing payroll exports already drop
  to SFTP.
- The `pulled_at` directory makes vintages first-class.
- Signed URLs handle the "we have one HR analyst with a CSV" case without
  needing the customer to stand up SFTP.

**Rejected: API push.**
Forces the customer's HRIS team to build an integration. Slows time-to-first-value
from days to months. Reasonable in v1.2 once we have a partner integration.

**Rejected: shared cloud-storage bucket.**
Operational simplicity for us, security review nightmare for them.

### 6.8 Hash-chained governance log (chosen) vs append-only file vs blockchain

**Chosen: SQLite + sha256 hash chain.**

Tamper-evident, single file, fits next to the warehouse, easy to back
up and verify. A break in the chain is a hard signal that something is
wrong.

**Rejected: blockchain / external timestamping service.**
We are not building a public ledger. The customer trusts us as their
vendor; the hash chain plus daily off-site backup is enough for any
realistic dispute.

**Rejected: append-only file with no integrity.**
Easy to silently truncate. Unacceptable for an audit artefact.

### 6.9 Hosting: EU-region VM (chosen) vs EU-region managed Kubernetes vs serverless

**Chosen: a small Linux VM in an EU region, behind Caddy, managed via
Ansible. Default: Hetzner FSN.**

- EU residency is contractual.
- Per-customer cost floor is €20–50/month, which leaves margin.
- One-page runbook to deploy a new customer.
- Backup is a daily `borgbackup` to a sibling EU bucket.

**Rejected: managed Kubernetes.**
Right answer at scale, wrong answer at one customer. The control plane
cost alone exceeds the per-customer revenue.

**Rejected: serverless (Lambda + RDS).**
DuckDB on Lambda is awkward; cold starts hurt the analyst flow; pricing
becomes unpredictable on heavy days.

## 7. Migration paths

These are the three migrations we expect to do post-v1.0. Each is
described in enough detail to verify v1.0 does not block them.

### 7.1 Templates → LLM-with-tools (v1.1)

- New module `domain/llm.py`. Calls Anthropic via the official SDK with
  prompt caching.
- Tool surface is the existing `domain/metrics.py` functions, exposed as
  Anthropic tool definitions.
- A `Verifier` class compares the model's emitted numbers against the
  tool-call results; on mismatch, a templated fallback runs.
- A new endpoint `/api/ask` switches to LLM mode behind a per-deployment
  flag. The templated path stays as the fallback.
- Audit fields written to the existing governance log.

**Why v1.0 doesn't block this:** the `domain/` layer is already pure and
typed; there is no monolith to refactor first.

### 7.2 Single-tenant DuckDB → multi-tenant Postgres (v1.1.5, on customer #3)

- Provision Postgres in the same EU region.
- Migrate dbt to use a Postgres adapter; the existing models are
  PG-compatible with the exception of two DuckDB-specific functions that
  we audit and replace pre-migration.
- One schema per customer (`tenant_<id>`).
- `repository/warehouse.py` gains a `tenant_id` parameter; everything
  upstream already passes a customer-scoped context, so the change is
  contained to the repository layer.
- DuckDB stays as the staging warehouse on each ingestion VM; only the
  serving warehouse moves to Postgres.

**Why v1.0 doesn't block this:** the API never embeds a customer id in a
SQL string; queries are parameterised and scoped through
`repository/warehouse.py`.

### 7.3 Templated PDF → custom-branded PDF per customer (v1.1)

- Each deployment ships a `templates/evidence_pack.<customer>.html` that
  inherits from the base template and overrides logo + colour variables.
- No code change in `domain/evidence.py`.

**Why v1.0 doesn't block this:** the PDF template is a single file with a
documented variable interface, not a hand-built PDF object graph.

### 7.4 Manual ingestion → HRIS partner integrations (v1.2)

Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, BambooHR, Personio. Each is a separate
adapter that lands files into the same `data/internal_raw/<customer_id>/`
directory the SFTP path uses today. The dbt staging models do not change.

## 8. Cross-cutting concerns

### 8.1 Performance budget

A single overview render must complete in under 800 ms p95 from a warm
process. Composition:

- Warehouse read: <100 ms (DuckDB on local disk, ~10 MB working set).
- Domain computation: <50 ms (pure Python over <1,000 rows).
- JSON serialisation + network: <100 ms over a EU LAN.
- Browser render: <500 ms including chart layout.

Anything slower is investigated; we do not optimise pre-emptively.

### 8.2 Cost envelope

Per customer per month, fully loaded:

| Line item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| EU VM (4 vCPU, 8 GB) | €30 |
| Backup storage (30-day, 50 GB) | €5 |
| Domain + TLS (Caddy / Let's Encrypt) | €0 |
| LLM cost (v1.1; templated v1.0 = €0) | <€20 |
| Total | ≤€55 |

This is the cost floor. Anything that pushes per-customer cost above €100
needs a justification.

### 8.3 Failure modes and fallbacks

| Failure | Detection | Fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Eurostat API down | `pull_eu_data.py` exits non-zero | Previous vintage stays live; freshness pill warns |
| Eurostat dimension renamed | Schema-baseline diff | Build fails, on-call paged, manual fix |
| dbt model fails | dbt exit code | Previous warehouse file stays mounted; staging warehouse rebuilt out-of-band |
| Customer CSV malformed | Validator error rate | Snapshot rejected, customer emailed with error report |
| OIDC IdP down | JWT verification fails | API returns 503 with retry-after; UI shows downtime page |
| Governance hash chain breaks | Verifier on read | Evidence-pack export blocked; on-call paged; manual investigation |
| WeasyPrint OOM on large pack | Process limit | JSON pack still served; PDF retried with reduced fonts |

### 8.4 Testing pyramid

- **Unit (domain/, policy/):** pure functions, exhaustive. Run in <5 s.
- **dbt tests:** schema, uniqueness, provenance, trust level, plus
  reconciliation against published headlines.
- **Contract tests:** every API response matches a JSON Schema in
  `contracts/`.
- **End-to-end smoke:** one test that loads the fixture warehouse, calls
  overview → ask → evidence → governance, and asserts hash-chain integrity
  on the resulting pack.
- **Frontend:** Storybook for primitives; Playwright for the four pages;
  axe in CI.

### 8.5 What we deliberately do not test

- Visual regression. We will fail those tests on legitimate design tweaks
  and waste the time they would save.
- Load tests. One concurrent user per deployment. We re-evaluate at
  customer #3.

## 10. Glossary

- **Evidence pack** — a signed JSON + PDF document that bundles the
  numeric claims, their provenance, and the warehouse vintage at the time
  of generation. The artefact a customer attaches to a regulatory filing.
- **Provenance** — the six required fields (publisher, dataset code, URL,
  vintage, pulled_at, licence) that travel with every numeric row.
- **Trust level** — the enum on every internal fact row controlling
  whether company-specific claims may be displayed or exported.
- **Vintage** — the date a source dataset was published or last refreshed
  by the publisher, distinct from `pulled_at` (when we fetched it).
- **Filter state** — the tuple `(country, geography, sector, period,
  benchmark_geography, benchmark_sector)` that selects rows for the UI.
- **MetricView** — the typed object passed from `domain/metrics.py` to
  `domain/narratives.py`. The only object that carries a number through
  the system.
